You will receive a transcript (interview, podcast, talk, or meeting). Your job is to extract compelling ideas for social media posts from it — for LinkedIn, X, Threads, Bluesky, Instagram, Facebook, or any similar network. Use the post types from the list below as a reference.

Follow these rules:

1. Generate as many ideas as you can. But prioritize quality over quantity. If the transcript only supports 3 strong ideas, stop at 3.
2. Never force a post type. If the transcript contains no genuine failure story, do not invent one just to fill the "mistakes and failures" slot. Only use post types that have real support in the source material.
3. A single transcript moment can map to more than one post type if the angles are genuinely different.
4. For each idea, name the platforms where it would work best and why in a few words — for example, professional lessons for LinkedIn, sharp one-liners for X or Threads, personal or visual stories for Instagram.
5. Do not write the posts themselves.
6. If you see a strong post idea that doesn't fit the list of post types below, add it too.
7. Keep your explanations short.
8. Output in markdown.

Post types:

Controversial opinion — A hot take on a topic grounded in your own experience. If everyone agrees with you, you are not saying anything interesting. The story behind the opinion is what makes it work.

Mistakes and failures — Stories about how you messed up and what you learned. Counterintuitive but proven: vulnerability builds more trust than bragging about success.

Consumer experiences — Something you noticed as a customer — a hidden fee, a confusing interface, a brilliant piece of service — turned into sharp analysis. Relatable and easy to react to.

Promoting others — Dedicate an entire post to saying something genuinely good about a person, creator, or business you admire. Underused, impossible to overdo, and great for relationships.

Life-changing moments — Major pivots: moving countries, switching careers, overcoming a crisis. These hero's journey stories resonate far beyond your niche.

Expertise and insights — Useful knowledge for your core audience. Does not need to be groundbreaking. Consistent, reliable insight builds authority over time.

Practical tips — A concrete how-to, checklist, or step-by-step method from the transcript that people can apply right away. Highly saveable and shareable.

Quotable moments — A single striking line, number, or turn of phrase that stands on its own. Works as a short post or as the hook for a longer one.

Celebrate your wins — Share even small victories. People enjoy cheering for progress at any scale.

Event recaps — Takeaways from a conference, meetup, or show. Mention speakers and people you met. Signals you are active in the community and opens doors. Use sparingly — tagging people who do not engage will hurt reach.

Your team and collaborators — Highlight the people behind the work: welcome a new colleague, thank a collaborator, say goodbye to someone leaving.

News with a personal twist — Share an update about your company, project, or work, but skip the press-release tone. Add why you are proud of it, what was hard about it, why it matters to you personally.

Share something personal — Hobbies, family, personal struggles. In moderation, it makes you three-dimensional and breaks down barriers with your audience.
